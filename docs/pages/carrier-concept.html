Concept render · rev B · not a layout

Runner early-warning carrier board

One through-hole board that is the whole node: a XIAO nRF52840 with Wio-SX1262 radio running MeshCore, a XIAO ESP32-S3 camera, a socketed charger and 5 V boost, the PIR on the back face, and an IR gate for night runs. Rev A, with the RAK19007 and RAK4631, comes first as the stepping stone.

What is on it

  • XIAO nRF52840 + Wio-SX1262The controller and LoRa radio, running MeshCore. Always on. Replaces the RAK19007 and RAK4631.
  • XIAO ESP32-S3 SenseCamera and person detection. Off between triggers, so the camera adds almost nothing to idle draw.
  • Adafruit bq24074 chargerSocketed, with the cell plugging into its own JST. Takes USB-C, a DC jack or a 6 V+ solar panel, which plugs into the keyed SOLAR connector on the carrier. Charges at 1 A, and keeps the load running with a dead battery.
  • Adafruit MiniBoost 5 VTPS61023, 5 V at up to 1 A. Its EN pin is driven by the nRF, and the product page says the output is fully disconnected when disabled.
  • PIR, back faceSoldered on the underside so it looks down-trail while the camera looks back at the aid station. Powered from the nRF's 3V3, output to D0. Arming is by firmware.
  • IR gate, for night opsLogic-level MOSFET driven from the camera's D1, switching the illuminator from the 5 V rail. The biggest load on the boost, so size the budget with it on.

Pins on the nRF side

XIAO pinUsed for
D1 · D2 · D3 · D4 · D5Radio: DIO1, reset, busy, chip select, RX enable
D8 · D9 · D10Radio SPI: clock, MISO, MOSI
D0PIR output (MeshCore calls it the user button)
D6Boost EN
D7UART receive, the camera's verdict. The link is one-way, so no transmit pin is needed.

Read from the MeshCore xiao_nrf52 build, not Seeed's pin-map image. MeshCore may probe I2C on D6 and D7 at startup, which could briefly disturb the UART. The radio pins are dimmed in the drawing.

Before layout starts

  1. Bench-check that the MiniBoost output really drops to zero when EN is low. The product page says it does; confirm with a meter.
  2. Stop the camera rail from running the cell flat: the MiniBoost keeps running down to roughly 2 V in, so the nRF firmware should refuse to enable it below a set battery voltage.
  3. The XIAO nRF52840's battery pads are on its back face, so the BAT feed shown here needs a wire or a different connection. Decide how the nRF takes cell power, and avoid a second charger starting on the same cell when USB is plugged into the XIAO.
  4. Check the bq24074 board's size, header names and whether the load output is on a header pin. Solar needs a 6 V or higher panel; the current RAK-class panel is 5.5 V at most. Also confirm how a panel connects to the module: the SOLAR connector here assumes DC-input pads on its header. If there are none, the panel goes to the DC jack through an adapter instead.
  5. Check the pin mapping against Seeed's docs, and what MeshCore does with D0, D6 and D7 at startup.
  6. Check the PIR's pin order and size, since AM312 modules differ. It works best 30 to 45 degrees off the trail axis, and a flat board mounts it square, so plan a stand-off or bracket.
  7. Plan the antenna: u.FL pigtail to an SMA, and keep the radio end of the board clear.
  8. Size the boost for the IR on: the MiniBoost gives about 800 mA at 3 V in and about 1.1 A at 3.5 V in. Then measure idle draw and size the cell, since the always-listening radio is likely the main drain.
